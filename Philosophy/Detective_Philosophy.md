# Daniel Mercer's Detective Philosophy

**Status:** LIVING CANON

## Purpose

This document tracks the principles Daniel carries, questions, revises, or learns. These are not skill-tree perks. They represent changes in how he observes people, evidence, institutions, and himself.

## Foundational Influences

- **Thomas Mercer — Truth:** What can be observed and proven?
- **Elaine Mercer — Context:** Who was this person before the event?
- **Rebecca Mercer — Consequence:** Who is affected if Daniel is right?
- **Mike Sullivan — Perspective:** What changes if the original assumption is reversed?
- **APD — Discipline:** Can the conclusion survive evidence, procedure, and scrutiny?
- **Romantic Partner — Reflection:** Who is Daniel becoming while pursuing the truth?

## Early Principles

- The evidence does not care what you believe.
- Contradictions are invitations to look again.
- A theory should be attacked before it is trusted.
- Resolution is not always understanding.

## Principles Developed Through the Story

The exact wording and case placement remain OPEN, but future lessons should include ideas such as:

- Evidence without context can mislead.
- Every witness is reliable about something, even when unreliable about the whole.
- The first theory is often the most comfortable rather than the most accurate.
- Justice without compassion can become punishment.
- Compassion without truth can become denial.
- Institutional boundaries can protect fairness while hiding patterns.
- Being correct does not remove responsibility for consequences.
- Knowing when to stop is part of investigation, not surrender.

## Design Rule

A relationship or reflective sequence may change Daniel's available reasoning, priorities, patience, or interpretation. It should not simply hand him a clue.

## Home Chapter Function

The Home chapter reconnects Daniel with first principles after professional or personal strain. It restores neither certainty nor a magical insight. It gives him the emotional and ethical perspective required to reconsider assumptions and see the whole person behind the evidence.
