# CHAR-006 — Daniel's Romantic Partner

**Status:** OPEN  
**Direction:** Slow-burn romance primarily outside case roles

## Core Distinction

Thomas, Elaine, Becca, and Mike are Daniel's roots. They help explain who he was and what formed him.

The romantic partner is a mirror. She reflects who Daniel is becoming.

## Narrative Functions

- Vulnerability
- Trust
- Emotional reciprocity
- Reflection
- Accountability
- The possibility of a future beyond cases

## Structural Role Across Multiple Cases

The relationship develops through the consequences of case decisions rather than isolated romance scenes.

Possible phases:

1. Curiosity
2. Understanding
3. Distance
4. Confrontation
5. Earned resolution

## Choice-Dependent Dynamics

The partner may influence Daniel positively or negatively depending on player behavior and the partner's own choices.

Examples:

- Healthy trust may help Daniel remain patient and balanced.
- Repeated dishonesty may make Daniel defensive or suspicious.
- Support may become enabling if neither person sets boundaries.
- Concern may become pressure or resentment.
- The partner may correctly challenge Daniel's obsession.
- The partner may also be wrong about a suspect or moral judgment.

Neither person should function as the permanent moral authority.

## Key Conflict

A detective survives professionally by questioning people. A relationship cannot survive if Daniel treats his partner like a witness or suspect.

Potential line:

> You're interviewing me.

## Major Design Rule

The romance must not reduce to affection points or a reward ending. It is a dynamic relationship in which both characters possess agency and both can grow, fail, harm, support, or leave.
