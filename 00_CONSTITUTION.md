# The Ashcroft Constitution

**Layer:** Immutable Canon  
**Status:** CANON

This document governs every future story, character, case, location, mechanic, and narrative choice.

## Article I — The Story Is About People

Crimes are catalysts. People are the story. The player should remember victims, families, friendships, and difficult choices—not only case solutions.

## Article II — Every Character Has Agency

No one exists solely to help Daniel Mercer. Every recurring character should have goals, fears, relationships, routines, and problems that continue when Daniel is absent.

## Article III — Ashcroft Is the Main Character

Ashcroft City existed before Daniel and will continue after him. Daniel is the lens through which the player discovers it. Each case reveals another layer of the city.

## Article IV — Truth Is Complicated

Evidence and logic matter, but justice, morality, and truth do not always align cleanly. Ambiguity should emerge naturally from human behavior, not from arbitrary obscurity.

## Article V — Growth Comes Through Perspective

Daniel rarely becomes a better detective because he acquires a gadget. He improves because relationships, mistakes, and reflection change how he thinks.

## Article VI — Every Major Character Has a Unique Narrative Function

If two major characters serve the same emotional or thematic function, they should be differentiated, merged, or removed.

Current functions:

- Thomas Mercer — Objective truth and disciplined observation
- Elaine Mercer — Memory, history, and context
- Rebecca Mercer — Compassion and human consequences
- Mike Sullivan — Perspective, alternative paths, and reversed assumptions
- Romantic Partner — Reflection, vulnerability, trust, and the person Daniel is becoming

## Article VII — Every Case Changes Something

Every case must alter at least one of the following:

- Daniel
- A relationship
- A neighborhood or institution
- The player's understanding of Ashcroft
- The meaning of truth, justice, or responsibility

No case exists purely as filler.

## Article VIII — Quiet Moments Matter

Sunday dinner, coffee at a garage, a walk through Eastside Flats, or cleaning an old workshop can carry as much emotional weight as a crime scene. The story must breathe.

## Article IX — Nothing Exists in Isolation

Not every crime belongs to the main conspiracy, but every meaningful action creates consequences. Cases should leave social, institutional, emotional, or moral ripples.

## Article X — Hope Must Exist

Ashcroft is imperfect, but it is worth fighting for. The story should not conclude that corruption makes goodness meaningless. It should show why good people still matter.

## Article XI — Intentional Scope

Every new location, organization, mechanic, or character must strengthen the existing world before expanding it. Ashcroft grows primarily by deepening relationships among familiar elements.

> Complexity should emerge from relationships, not from the number of systems.

## Article XII — The Greater Mystery Emerges

The main conspiracy is a distributed network of interests, dependencies, and ordinary wrongdoing rather than a single secret society that controls everything. It should feel inevitable in hindsight and be discovered through believable connections among cases and ecosystems.

Institutions may miss the whole while competently handling their assigned parts. The mystery asks not only who acted, but why no one could see the complete pattern.

## Core Design Pillars

### 1. The Detective

The player investigates scenes, gathers evidence, interviews people, constructs timelines, connects clues, and reaches conclusions.

**Central question:** How do I solve the case?

### 2. The Human

Daniel's family, friendships, romance, failures, and reflection shape the person doing the investigating.

**Central question:** Who is Daniel becoming?

### 3. The City

Every case reveals Ashcroft's history, institutions, neighborhoods, class divisions, and culture.

**Central question:** Why is this city the way it is?

### 4. The Truth

Cases explore evidence, certainty, guilt, justice, compassion, and the consequences of being right.

**Central question:** What does finding the truth actually mean?

## Evaluation Test for New Ideas

Before approving a new idea, ask:

1. Does it challenge Daniel as a detective?
2. Does it reveal or change Daniel as a person?
3. Does it reveal something meaningful about Ashcroft?
4. Does it explore truth, justice, morality, or consequence?
5. Does it duplicate another character or system's function?
6. Has it earned its place, or is it present only because it seems cool?
