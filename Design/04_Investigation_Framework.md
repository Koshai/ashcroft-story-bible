# Investigation Framework

**Status:** CANON

## Cognitive Pillars

### Observe
Notice what is present, absent, changed, staged, or inconsistent.

### Organize
Give raw information structure through timelines, relationships, locations, and categories.

### Hypothesize
Create competing explanations rather than committing immediately to the first comfortable answer.

### Challenge
Actively search for information that could disprove the preferred theory.

### Understand
Explain not only who acted, but how, why, and with what consequences.

## Information Confidence Ladder

1. **Observation** — Something Daniel directly perceives.
2. **Information** — A statement or record that may be incomplete or false.
3. **Lead** — Information worth pursuing.
4. **Evidence** — Information meaningfully corroborated or physically supported.
5. **Verified Fact** — A claim supported strongly enough to be treated as established.
6. **Conclusion** — An explanation constructed from verified facts.

Information is not automatically a clue. It becomes evidentially meaningful through context, corroboration, and reasoning.

## Reasonable Confidence

A case may advance when the player's current theory is the strongest explanation supported by available verified evidence, even when uncertainty remains.

The detective provides understanding. Police, prosecutors, courts, clients, and society determine what happens afterward.

## Mercer Principle

> A good detective does not only search for evidence that proves a theory right. A good detective searches for evidence that could prove it wrong.
